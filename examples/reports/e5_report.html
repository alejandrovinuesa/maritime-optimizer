Summary

Two routings of the same LR2 LNG-fuelled tanker in 2026 were compared. Via Suez costs €2,470,421 for 10,185 nm and 37.8 days. Via the Cape costs €2,565,171 for 14,594 nm and 52.8 days, which is €94,750 (3.8 %) more. Both run at a mean 11.64 kn. The Cape route avoids €650,000 in canal tolls, but this saving is more than offset by the extra 15.0 days: time-charter cost rises by €488,375, fuel by €220,938 (340 t more LNG) and EU ETS by €35,437. Time charter is the largest cost item on both routes (49.7 % via Suez, 66.9 % via the Cape). Both voyages are extra-EU, so 50 % of tank-to-wake GHG is covered by EU ETS. Both comply with FuelEU Maritime (83.83 against a limit of 89.34 gCO2e/MJ) and both rate CII A for this voyage. ISO 14083 intensity is identical on both routes (2.142 gCO2e/tkm). The longer route raises absolute emissions, not intensity per tonne-km.

Findings

  • Suez is cheaper by €94,750 (3.8 %). Total cost is €2,470,421 via Suez and €2,565,171 via the Cape. The Cape route adds 4,408 nm and 15.0 transit days. Its €650,000 toll saving is outweighed by higher time-charter (+€488,375), fuel (+€220,938) and EU ETS (+€35,437) costs.
  • Time charter is the main cost driver on both routes. The time-charter equivalent is €1,228,058 (49.7 %) via Suez and €1,716,434 (66.9 %) via the Cape. On the Suez route the next items are canal tolls at €650,000 (26.3 %) and fuel at €510,483 (20.7 %). EU ETS is a small share on both routes: 3.3 % via Suez and 4.6 % via the Cape.
  • Methane slip raises the EU ETS base above physical CO2. Via Suez, tank-to-wake GHG is 2,519.4 t CO2e against 2,123.0 t physical CO2. Via the Cape, the figures are 3,609.7 t against 3,041.9 t. The gap comes from LNG engine methane slip, which counts in the 2026 EU ETS base at GWP-100 of 28. Because both voyages are extra-EU, allowances cover 50 % of this figure: €81,879 via Suez and €117,316 via the Cape.
  • Longer route raises absolute emissions, not intensity. The Cape route burns 340 t more fuel (1,125.3 t against 785.4 t) and emits 1,090 t more tank-to-wake CO2e. Ship, fuel and speed are the same on both routes. As a result, CII attained (1.895), FuelEU intensity (83.83 gCO2e/MJ) and ISO 14083 intensity (2.142 gCO2e/tkm) are identical. The comparison lists Suez as lowest ISO 14083, but the two values are tied (0.0 % difference).
  • FuelEU Maritime and CII positions are comfortable. LNG's well-to-wake intensity of 83.83 gCO2e/MJ is below the 2026 limit of 89.34 gCO2e/MJ, so there is no deficit and no penalty on either route. CII attained is 1.895 against a required 3.927, rating A, with no speed cap applied.

Recommendations

  • Route via Suez on the current assumptions. It saves €94,750 and 15.0 days and avoids 1,090 t of tank-to-wake CO2e.
  • Before fixing the routing, re-run the comparison with the actual time-charter rate, the Suez toll quote and any Red Sea war-risk premium. The 3.8 % margin depends mainly on time-charter and toll levels.
  • Ask the Suez Canal Authority or the agent about toll rebates available for this ship type and route, since tolls are 26.3 % of the Suez voyage cost.
  • Include an EU ETS cost pass-through clause in the charter party so that allowance costs follow the charterer.
  • Assess engine methane-slip reduction (engine tuning or a low-slip engine configuration), since slip raises the EU ETS base above physical CO2.
  • Bank the FuelEU Maritime compliance surplus from operating below the intensity limit, or use it in a pool with fleet vessels in deficit.

Caveats

  • Fuel, EUA, time-charter and toll prices are illustrative; actual quotes may change the €94,750 margin and possibly the ranking.
  • The vessel is a generic LR2 LNG tanker model, not a specific hull and engine.
  • Calm-water performance is assumed. Weather, currents and the seasonal sea state around the Cape and in the Indian Ocean are not modelled.
  • Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb security risk, war-risk insurance and possible delays at Suez are not included in the costs.
  • The CII and FuelEU figures cover a single voyage. The legal CII rating and the FuelEU compliance balance are determined on annual, per-ship figures.
  • EU ETS coverage assumes an extra-EU voyage at 50 %. Algeciras is treated as a waypoint, not a port call; an EU port call would change the coverage split.
